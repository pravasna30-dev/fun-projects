Big Ideas

THINK
1

There's no free lunch

States still need money for schools and roads. A state with no income tax usually collects more some other way: sales tax, property tax, or taxes on tourists and oil.

2

Every state has property tax

Trick question! All 50 states have property tax. It mostly pays for local schools. What changes is the rate: from 0.31% in Hawaii to 1.79% in Illinois.

3

Percent ≠ dollars

A 1% property tax on a $300,000 house is $3,000 a year. A 7% sales tax on a $100 game is $7. Always turn the percent into dollars!

4

Top rate ≠ your rate

“Graduated” states tax each slice of income at a higher rate as income goes up. New York's 10.9% top rate only hits income above $25 million. Most people pay much less.

5

Different judges, different winners

WalletHub says Wyoming is the #1 place to retire. Bankrate picked New Hampshire. They weigh weather, health care and cost differently. Rankings are opinions built from data.

6

Big economy ≠ rich people

GDP is everything a state makes and sells in a year. California is #1 because it has the most people. A small state can still have a high income per person.

Talk About It

CLASS
  1. Texas has no income tax. Find two taxes on the map where Texas is above average. Why might that be?
  2. You're buying an iPhone and you live near the Oregon border in Washington. What would you do? Is that fair to Washington?
  3. Grandma wants to retire somewhere warm with low taxes. Use the map to pick 3 states for her, and explain your choice.
  4. Would you rather pay a high sales tax (everyone pays, even tourists) or a high income tax (people who earn more pay more)? Why?
  5. Play the game with your priorities, then trade with a friend. Did you get the same #1? Why not?

Word Wall

WORDS
Income taxA slice of the money you earn at a job, paid to the state (and the country).
Flat taxEveryone pays the same percent, no matter how much they earn.
Graduated taxThe percent goes up as income goes up, one “bracket” at a time.
Sales taxExtra money added at the register when you buy stuff. States and cities can each add some.
Property taxA yearly tax on the value of a house or land. It pays for local schools, fire departments and parks.
Tax burdenAll state and local taxes people pay, as a percent of all income in the state.
GDPGross Domestic Product: the total value of everything made and sold in a place in one year.
Excise taxA tax on one specific thing, like gasoline. It's usually hidden inside the price.

Where the numbers come from

  • Income-tax rates (tax year 2026): Tax Foundation, Facts & Figures 2026, Table 11 (as of Mar 23 2026), updated for laws signed later: Georgia 4.99% (HB 463), South Carolina 1.99% / 5.21% (H.4216), Utah 4.45%.
  • Sales-tax rates: Tax Foundation, State and Local Sales Tax Rates, Midyear 2026 (July 1 2026). Local rates are population-weighted averages; your town may differ.
  • Property tax (effective rate on owner-occupied homes, 2024), total tax burden (2022) and gas tax (Jan 2026): Tax Foundation, Facts & Figures 2026, Tables 33, 2 and 22.
  • GDP: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, SAGDP1, current-dollar GDP for 2025 (released Apr 8 2026).
  • Retirement rank: WalletHub, Best & Worst States to Retire 2026 (D.C. not ranked).
  • iPhone prices: Apple U.S. prices after the Sept 9 2026 price change. Price × (1 + average combined sales tax). Doesn't include carrier fees.
  • Map: us-atlas (U.S. Census Bureau boundaries, Albers USA projection). Alaska and Hawaii aren't drawn to scale.

For learning, not tax advice. Real taxes depend on where exactly you live, your income and lots of special rules.
